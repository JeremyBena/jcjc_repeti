Axe 1 – Word-for-word and thematic repetition effects on truth judgments

We will test the effect of repetition (word-for-word repetition vs. thematic repetition vs. new statements) on truth judgments (assessed in a binary “true”/“false” truth judgment task) of adult participants on trivia and conspiracy statements. The basic procedure is an extension of the typical truth effect procedure (Henderson et al., 2022; Unkelbach et al., 2019). Experiment 1 will use trivia statements, and Experiment 2 will use conspiracy statements. These experiments are replications and extensions of prior research we conducted on the effect of word-for-word repetition on trivia and conspiracy statements (Béna et al., 2023).

Based on conceptual fluency (Vogel et al., 2020) and fuzzy-trace theory accounts of the truth effect (Béna et al., 2019; Edelson et al., 2024), thematic repetition is enough to obtain a truth effect similar in size to the truth effect with word-for-word repetition. From this perspective, retrieving the gist of a statement, not verbatim representations, is key to the truth effect. However, past research does not allow clear conclusions regarding the effect of repetition’s format on the size of the truth effect because (1) they were not sufficiently powered to detect such differences and (2) contradictory results were obtained (Begg & Armour, 1991; Gratton et al., 2024; Pillai & Fazio, 2025; Silva et al., 2017). In addition, no such test was conducted with conspiracy statements.

If retrieving the gist of statements contributes to the truth effect regardless of the retrieval of verbatim representations, we predict that the truth effect should be similar in magnitude for word-for-word and thematic repetition. However, if retrieving verbatim representations also contributes to the truth effect, the truth effect should be larger for word-for-word than thematic repetition (because items repeated identically are better cues to verbatim representations than items repeated thematically, Stahl & Klauer, 2008). We will conduct experiments securing enough statistical power to detect a small effect of repetition’s format on the truth effect. In addition to frequentist analyses, we will use Bayesian analyses to quantify the support for the hypothesis of no effect against the hypothesis of a larger truth effect for word-for-word vs. thematic repetition.
